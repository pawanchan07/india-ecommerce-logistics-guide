# The Parcel Ledger — how India's e-commerce logistics actually works

I am Pawan. I built this while teaching myself the Indian e-commerce delivery industry,
because I wanted to understand what really happens between someone tapping "buy" on a D2C
site and a parcel arriving in a Tier-3 town. The public writing on it is either investor
commentary or company PR. Neither tells you how the thing physically moves, who pays whom,
or where the margin actually sits.

## What is inside and how to use it

**`india-ecommerce-logistics.html`** — open it in a browser. Self-contained, no install,
no server.

It follows the parcel: first mile, sortation, line haul, last mile, returns — and the
money moving alongside it: COD, RTO, and the unit economics underneath. It is built to be
explored rather than read front to back, so use the navigation and go where your question
is.

The other two files are the honest part of this repo:

- **`deep-research-guide.txt`** — the research brief I wrote before any of this existed
- **`output-guide.txt`** — the instruction that the result be something you explore, not a
  wall of text

I kept both in the repo because if you want to do this for another industry, the prompts
are more reusable than my output is.

## Who this might help

Product and operations people entering Indian e-commerce or logistics. Founders working
out what shipping will really cost them. Anyone who wants a mental model of the industry
that survives contact with someone who actually works in it.

## How I built it

Deep research with Claude against the brief in `deep-research-guide.txt`, then built as a
single interactive HTML file. I wrote the brief and the structure and did the editing; the
research volume is AI-assisted and it would be dishonest to imply otherwise.

## An honest limit

This is an outsider's synthesis of public sources, not insider knowledge, and it is not
about any one company. Treat the numbers as directional and verify anything load-bearing
before you build a plan on it.
